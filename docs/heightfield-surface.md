# Retained heightfield surface geometry

`heightfield::PreparedHeightfield3` consumes explicit increasing X columns, Z rows, row-major f64 heights and an optional row-major active-cell mask. Coordinates can be nonuniform. It validates lengths, finite values, increasing spacing, representable differences and triangle identifiers. Enabled cells prepare their two unit +Y normals once. The data is immutable after construction.

Each cell has A=(-X,-Z), B=(+X,-Z), C=(-X,+Z), D=(+X,+Z). Even `(cell_x + cell_z)` parity uses ACD/ADB; odd parity uses ACB/BCD. This matches Collision Lab's blue-noise mesh subdivision without importing generation, seeds, simulation or visualization into the kernel. Omitted cells remove both triangles; partial-cell holes and arbitrary winding are outside this contract.

`sample(x,z)` projects vertically onto this surface. It returns position, normal, stable triangle ID and actual query work; invalid/non-finite input returns attempted work with its error. Outside the closed coordinate extent and within a hole it returns no surface. Internal grid edges prefer the positive-side cell. If it is omitted, an enabled -X, -Z or -X/-Z neighbor at the exact grid boundary may supply the surface, in that order. The maximum exterior edges are included. Within a cell, the first triangle owns its diagonal. Heights are continuous across shared enabled edges; normals can legitimately change across a sharp edge.

Binary search visits at most logarithmically many coordinates. An ordinary query inspects one cell and one triangle, reuses one normal, does no geometry preparation and allocates nothing. Hole-boundary fallback inspects at most four cells. IDs retain their original slots when a cell is omitted. `triangle_vertices` returns no result for a hole or an invalid ID. Preparation reports computed triangles and vector payload capacities, excluding object/allocator overhead.

Finite inputs are not an arbitrary-magnitude promise. Unrepresentable coordinate/height differences, degenerate normals caused by extreme ratios, or non-finite interpolated heights return errors. Scaled cross products retain very small and large flat cells; independent fixtures cover 1e-300..1e150 flat scales, negative coordinates near 1e9 and 1e-6/1/1e6 nonuniform data. No saturating physical casts or silent coordinate clamping are used.

Five Cargo tests cover 1536 deterministic seeded queries against an independent exhaustive mesh-plane/barycentric oracle, analytic plane normals, repeated identity, shared edges/vertices and boundaries, holes, invalid data and numerical limits. The existing allocation suite runs 8192 retained 65×65 samples with zero allocations/reallocations, exactly 8192 triangle tests and normal reuses, and at most 16 coordinate comparisons per sample. That fixture prepares 8192 normals and retains 231,448 vector-payload bytes.

The existing `primitive3` Divan suite includes a complete prepared-sample workload; fixture construction is outside the timed query. Timing is advisory and does not establish a speedup over Collision Lab's previous f32 indexed implementation. This mathematical sample supplies no swept-volume collision, ray result, signed body separation, movement/step policy, support motion, wake behavior or world/chunk lifecycle. Physics Engine owns those semantics and adoption under physics-engine#195/#194.
